Executive Summary

Brand-name answer (your question): Yes. Osilodrostat phosphate is traded commercially under the brand ISTURISA® (film-coated tablets). It was discovered by Novartis as LCI699; Recordati licensed global rights from Novartis in July 2019 for US$390M upfront [20], and the US NDA holder is now Recordati Rare Diseases [11]. In your shipment screenshot the product is described only by INN ("OSILODROSTAT PHOSPHATE (ADRENOCORTICAL SUPPRESSANT)") — no brand appears in the description — but the shippers include the brand owners (Novartis Pharma AG, Recordati Rare Diseases) and two specialty clinical/named-patient distributors, so these consignments are almost certainly branded ISTURISA® tablets or brand-origin API, not generics.

Near-term opportunity lies in reference standards / impurities, CRO process R&D, comparator & named-patient supply, and pre-positioning a generic API DMF for post-patent markets.

Product Overview & Identity

Osilodrostat phosphate is the 1:1 phosphoric-acid salt of osilodrostat, a small non-steroidal molecule containing a fused 6,7-dihydro-5H-pyrrolo[1,2-c]imidazole ring attached at a single (R)-stereocentre to a 3-fluoro-4-cyanophenyl group. It potently inhibits 11β-hydroxylase (CYP11B1), the last enzyme of cortisol biosynthesis, and also aldosterone synthase (CYP11B2) [1]. Originally developed for hypertension/heart failure, those programs were discontinued by Novartis in January 2013 and it was repositioned for Cushing's disease [2]. Commercially, it is the active ingredient of ISTURISA® — a rare-disease specialty drug with high price, low volume and strong growth.

Industry Applications, End-Products & Application Market Sizes

A. Pharmaceutical — Endogenous Cushing's Syndrome / Cushing's Disease (core application)

Osilodrostat is a chronic oral therapy (start 2 mg BID, up to 30 mg BID) used when pituitary/adrenal surgery is not an option or not curative. US approval came in March 2020 for Cushing's disease; in April 2025 FDA expanded the label to endogenous hypercortisolemia in adults with Cushing's syndrome [10]. EU approval (January 2020) already covered endogenous Cushing's syndrome [2].
